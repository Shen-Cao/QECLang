\section{Fixed-encoder channel recovery}

For a fixed encoding isometry $V\in\mathbb C^{D\times K}$ and noise Kraus
operators $N_a\in\mathbb C^{E\times D}$, set $A_a=N_aV$. A recovery to
the logical space has Kraus operators $R_b\in\mathbb C^{K\times E}$.
For the maximally mixed logical input, entanglement fidelity is
\begin{equation}
 F_e=\frac{1}{K^2}\sum_{a,b}|\operatorname{Tr}(R_bA_a)|^2.
\end{equation}
Optimizing recovery for a fixed encoding and noise model is a
semidefinite program~\cite{fletcher2007}; this objective does not assert
worst-case fidelity or optimize the encoding simultaneously.

Use column-stacked vectorization, ordered as received space tensor
logical output, and define
\begin{equation}
 J=\sum_b\operatorname{vec}_F(R_b)\operatorname{vec}_F(R_b)^\dagger,
 \qquad
 C=\frac{1}{K^2}\sum_a
 \operatorname{vec}_F(A_a^\dagger)\operatorname{vec}_F(A_a^\dagger)^\dagger.
\end{equation}
The conjugation is essential:
$\operatorname{vec}_F(A_a^\dagger)^\dagger\operatorname{vec}_F(R_b)
=\operatorname{Tr}(R_bA_a)$. Thus the primal and dual are
\begin{align}
 \max_J\quad &\operatorname{Tr}(CJ)
 &\text{subject to }&J\succeq0,\quad\operatorname{Tr}_{K}J=I_E,\\
 \min_{Y=Y^\dagger}\quad &\operatorname{Tr}Y
 &\text{subject to }&Y\otimes I_K-C\succeq0.
\end{align}
Indeed the Lagrangian is
$\operatorname{Tr}Y+\operatorname{Tr}[(C-Y\otimes I_K)J]$.
The primal has a strictly feasible point $I_E\otimes I_K/K$, so the
finite-dimensional formulation has strong duality.

The optional CVXPY backend implements this formulation for a fixed subspace
encoder and numerically validated Kraus noise, solving primal and dual
problems separately. It rejects unprepared gauge subsystems. A subsystem
objective would require an explicitly specified gauge preparation. Treating all logical--gauge columns as protected
logical states would change the subsystem objective. It is a dense
small-system analysis, with Choi dimension $EK$; it does not supply a
sparse qLDPC decoder or an extraction circuit.

Numerical results must retain trace-preservation and Hermiticity
residuals, minimum primal/dual-slack eigenvalues, solver statuses, and
objective gaps. Solver values within tolerance are numerical evidence,
not rigorous arithmetic bounds. If a recovery is extracted by spectral
truncation, its trace preservation and achieved fidelity must be checked
again. The implementation retains the raw Choi candidate without implicit repair.
Its numerical-optimal status requires both solver optimality reports,
independently acceptable residuals, and a small numerical gap; primal-only,
inaccurate, invalid, and solver-error states remain distinct. Dense
allocation guards do not bound solver workspace or elapsed time. Neither
an optimum channel nor its Kraus decomposition supplies a fault-tolerant
circuit realization.
