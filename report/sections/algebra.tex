\section{Exact binary algebra}

\subsection{Pauli and subsystem data}
Represent a Pauli by $i^p X^x Z^z$ with $x,z\in\F^n$ and
$p\in\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$. Its support weight is
$|\operatorname{supp}(x)\cup\operatorname{supp}(z)|$, not the Hamming
weight of the concatenated vector. The commutation pairing is
\begin{equation}
 \langle(x,z),(x',z')\rangle_s=x\cdot z'+z\cdot x'.
\end{equation}
A stabilizer code requires Hermitian commuting generators whose group
excludes $-I$. Support orthogonality alone does not check the last
condition. If the support space $S$ has dimension $s$, the code has
$k=n-s$ logical qubits. Nontrivial Pauli logical operators lie in
$S^\perp\setminus S$~\cite{gottesman1997}.

For a phase-free gauge support space $G$, let $S=G\cap G^\perp$,
$g=\dim G$, and $s=\dim S$. Then
\begin{equation}
 r=(g-s)/2,\qquad k=n-(g+s)/2.
\end{equation}
The dressed and bare logical quotients are respectively
$S^\perp/G$ and $G^\perp/S$~\cite{poulin2005}. Gauge support determines
these parameters; specifying a particular embedded subsystem also requires
consistent eigenvalues for the center. Gauge fixing is an additional
operation with choices, not a representation identity.

\subsection{The CSS chain dialect}
Let $H_X\in\F^{m_X\times n}$ and $H_Z\in\F^{m_Z\times n}$ with
$H_XH_Z^T=0$. We fix positive pure-X and pure-Z generators and the complex
\begin{equation}
 C_2=\F^{m_Z}\xrightarrow{d_2=H_Z^T}
 C_1=\F^n\xrightarrow{d_1=H_X}C_0=\F^{m_X}.
\end{equation}
The bases are part of the data. For a Pauli error $(x,z)$ the two
syndromes are $H_Xz$ and $H_Zx$. The logical spaces are
\begin{equation}
 L_Z=\ker H_X/\row H_Z,\qquad
 L_X=\ker H_Z/\row H_X.
\end{equation}

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}
The number of logical qubits is
$k=n-\rank H_X-\rank H_Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The boundary condition gives $\im d_2\subseteq\ker d_1$.
Rank--nullity gives
$\dim\ker d_1=n-\rank H_X$, while
$\dim\im d_2=\rank H_Z$. Taking the quotient proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\end{samepage}

For $k>0$, define
\begin{align}
 d_Z&=\min\{\wt z:z\in\ker H_X\setminus\row H_Z\},\\
 d_X&=\min\{\wt x:x\in\ker H_Z\setminus\row H_X\}.
\end{align}
The Pauli distance is $\min(d_X,d_Z)$: a nontrivial mixed logical must
have at least one nontrivial component, and either pure sector is itself
an allowed logical. This is a metric statement about based homology and
cohomology, not just their dimensions. The software uses infinite
\emph{logical} distance for $k=0$, since the nontrivial-logical sets are
empty; other conventions for stabilizer-state distance exist.

Changing check generators preserves row spaces but can make their
supports dense. Changing arbitrary chain bases preserves abstract
homology but can change Hamming distance. Consequently a compiler must
track logical equivalence, metric preservation, and measurement
presentation as different properties.
